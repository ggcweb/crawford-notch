Design Patterns & Guidelines for Hotel and Campground Websites
Core UX Principles
First impression is everything. Hospitality sites sell an experience before the booking. Every design decision should evoke the feeling of being there.

1. Visual Design

Hero imagery is king. Use full-width, high-quality photos or video of the property. Show the best room, the view, the campfire — not a logo.
Atmosphere over information. Lead with mood; let details follow. Warm, earthy tones work for campgrounds; clean whites and rich neutrals for hotels.
Consistent typography. One serif for headings (elegance/nature) + one clean sans-serif for body. Avoid more than two typefaces.
Whitespace is your friend. Crowded layouts feel budget. Let content breathe.
Dark mode consideration. Campground sites especially benefit from dark backgrounds that evoke night skies and nature.


2. Booking Flow

Sticky "Book Now" CTA — always visible in the header, no matter how far the user scrolls.
Date picker front and center — availability and pricing should be immediately accessible, ideally above the fold.
Minimal friction. Keep checkout to 3 steps maximum: dates → room/site selection → payment.
Real-time availability. Never let a user reach the end of the flow to discover their dates are unavailable.
Price transparency. Show taxes and fees early. Hidden fees at checkout are the #1 cause of cart abandonment in hospitality.
Guest count selector. Pair it with the date picker so users know upfront if capacity works for them.


3. Navigation Structure
A clean, flat hierarchy works best:
Home → Accommodations → Amenities → Location/Map → Gallery → Contact/Book

Keep the primary nav to 5–6 items max.
Use a mega-menu only if you have multiple property types (cabins, tent sites, glamping, etc.).
"Book Now" should be a button, not a nav link — visually distinct.


4. Content & Copywriting

Lead with benefits, not features. "Wake up to mountain views" beats "Mountain-facing rooms available."
Scannable room/site cards with: photo, name, capacity, price, key amenities, and a CTA.
Social proof above the fold. Star rating + review count near the top builds immediate trust.
Local area content. "Things to do nearby" improves SEO and helps guests self-sell the trip.
For campgrounds: be very explicit about hookups, water access, pet policies, and fire rules — these are decision-critical.


5. Photography & Media

Minimum 10–15 unique property photos — exterior, rooms/sites, bathrooms, amenities, views, common areas.
Show real context: people enjoying the space, not just empty rooms.
Use a lightbox gallery so users can browse without leaving the page.
Virtual tours or 360° photos dramatically increase bookings for premium properties.
Optimize all images for web (WebP format, lazy loading).


6. Trust & Credibility Signals

Verified review badges (Google, TripAdvisor, Hipcamp, Booking.com)
Security badges on payment pages (SSL, PCI compliance)
Awards, press mentions, or certifications
Clear cancellation policy — link it near every "Book" button
Phone number visible in the header (huge trust signal for older demographics)


7. Mobile-First Design

Over 60% of hospitality browsing happens on mobile. Design mobile first, scale up.
Date pickers must be touch-friendly (large tap targets, native mobile calendar inputs).
Sticky bottom bar on mobile with "Check Availability" CTA.
Images must load fast — compress aggressively.
Click-to-call phone number.


8. Campground-Specific Patterns

Interactive site map — let guests pick their specific campsite visually (huge differentiator).
Filter by hookup type: electric only, full hookup, dry camping, tent-only.
Pet policy prominently displayed (it's a dealbreaker for many).
Amenity icons for each site: fire ring, picnic table, shade, proximity to bathhouse.
Seasonal pricing and blackout date visibility.
"What to bring" checklist as content — great for SEO and guest experience.


9. Hotel-Specific Patterns

Room comparison table for properties with multiple room types.
Loyalty/member pricing toggle if applicable.
Add-ons during checkout: early check-in, parking, breakfast, spa packages.
Floor plan or view selector for premium rooms.
Prominently featured concierge/contact option for group bookings.


10. Performance & SEO

Page load under 2.5 seconds (Core Web Vitals matter for Google ranking).
Structured data markup (Hotel, LodgingBusiness, CampingVehicle) for rich search results.
Location-specific landing pages if you have multiple properties.
Schema markup for reviews and pricing.
Google Maps embed on the location page with directions.
